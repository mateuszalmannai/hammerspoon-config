    Paired Comparison Guide

    About Paired Comparison

    Paired Comparison is a decision-making technique that helps you prioritize options by comparing them two at a time. Instead of trying to rank all options at once (which can be overwhelming), you make simple "A or B" choices.

    This method reduces cognitive load and reveals your true preferences by forcing direct comparisons. It's particularly useful when you have multiple options that seem equally appealing.

    How to Use

    1. Add Options: Enter 2-10 options you want to compare
    2. Start Comparing: Click "Start Comparing" when ready
    3. Make Choices: For each pair, click the option you prefer
    4. Skip if Needed: Use "Skip" if you can't decide (counts as a tie)
    5. View Results: See your options ranked by win rate

    Total Pairs: For n options, you'll make n(n-1)/2 comparisons.

    Example: 5 options = 10 comparisons, 10 options = 45 comparisons

    Best Practices

    • Be Consistent: Use the same criteria for all comparisons
    • Decide Quickly: Your first instinct is often most accurate
    • Use Specific Options: "Toyota Camry" is better than "a sedan"
    • Limit Options: 5-7 options is optimal; more than 10 becomes tedious
    • Skip Sparingly: Too many skips reduce the quality of results
    • Run Multiple Times: If results seem off, run a new comparison to verify
    • Consider Context: Results reflect your preferences at this moment
