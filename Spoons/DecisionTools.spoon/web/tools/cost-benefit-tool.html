      Cost-Benefit Analysis Guide

      About Cost-Benefit Analysis

      Cost-Benefit Analysis (CBA) is a systematic approach to evaluating decisions by comparing total expected costs against total expected benefits. It helps you determine whether an investment, project, or decision is financially worthwhile.

      This tool quantifies both one-time and recurring costs and benefits, automatically annualizing recurring items to provide a clear year-over-year comparison.

      Key Metrics

      Total Costs (Annualized)

      The sum of all costs. One-time costs are shown as-is, while recurring costs are annualized (monthly x 12).

      Total Benefits (Annualized)

      The sum of all benefits. One-time benefits are shown as-is, while recurring benefits are annualized (monthly x 12).

      Net Benefit

      Formula: Total Benefits - Total Costs

      A positive net benefit indicates the decision creates value; a negative value suggests costs outweigh benefits.

      Benefit-Cost Ratio (BCR)

      Formula: Total Benefits / Total Costs

      BCR > 1: Benefits exceed costs (favorable)

      BCR = 1: Benefits equal costs (break-even)

      BCR < 1: Costs exceed benefits (unfavorable)

      Return on Investment (ROI)

      Formula: (Net Benefit / Total Costs) x 100%

      Positive ROI: Investment gains value

      Negative ROI: Investment loses value

      How to Use

      1. Add Items: Enter a description, amount, and select whether it's a cost or benefit
      2. Set Category: Choose One-time or Recurring
      3. Set Frequency: For recurring items, choose Monthly or Yearly
      4. Review Metrics: The summary cards update automatically
      5. Make Decision: Use BCR and ROI to evaluate the decision

      Best Practices

      • Be Comprehensive: Include all relevant costs and benefits, even if estimates
      • Be Conservative: When uncertain, overestimate costs and underestimate benefits
      • Consider Intangibles: Try to quantify non-monetary factors like time savings or risk reduction
      • Use Consistent Timeframes: Compare costs and benefits over the same period
      • Review Assumptions: Document why you assigned specific values

      Common Use Cases

      • Software or equipment purchases
      • Hiring decisions
      • Process improvement initiatives
      • Make vs. buy decisions
      • Project prioritization
      • Vendor comparisons
